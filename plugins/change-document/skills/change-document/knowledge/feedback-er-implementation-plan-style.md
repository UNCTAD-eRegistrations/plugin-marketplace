Frank's rules for implementation-plan documents, distilled from the line-by-line review of the Lesotho beneficial-owners plan (2026-07-22). A template skill will freeze these once the BO review closes; until then this memory is the source.

**Why:** the first drafts failed three ways: they described configuration states instead of the service's logic ("the explainer is switched off" — "I don't care"), they used invented words ("saver", "publisher", "workspace", "table"), and they carried one wrong generalized claim ("a bot rewriting erases"). Each earned a correction.

**How to apply:**
- **Write the logic, not the configuration.** What the service does, in the order it does it; each element earns its place by its role in the flow. Configuration states belong in the phases that change them.
- **His categories**: the screens · the bots · the database. "Where we stand" and "The implementation goal" both use them. No verdict section.
- **Vocabulary is law** (the eRegistrations Glossary + Rosetta Stone): grid (never table), data bot / internal bot, publishing to draft or live, the register is a GDB database, input/output mappings. Never generalize platform behavior from one case (a bot does not always read a grid).
- **Every claim evidence-tagged** (live pull date, export date, Frank's word); a wrong claim is removed loudly, not patched quietly. Bot names given in prose ("NP owns at least 10 of shares"), UUIDs only in collapsed builder notes.
- **Drawn sections** (the capital-shares grammar): the form as the applicant will see it, fields tagged radio/dropdown/read-only/new, kept fields as one quiet legend line. Every applicant text written out in full, leaflet spirit ([[leaflet-editing-spirit]] file in eR knowledge).
- **UI**: all sections and phases collapsed by default; phase subsections lettered (0a, 0b…), collapsible, accent-colored; title = subject dark + ", implementation plan" grey; one quiet grey for gates and tags (no amber family); index chips on top; token audit 0.
- **The verdict loop**: he reviews line by line in the browser; every correction is applied to the page the same hour, then the surroundings re-read ([[feedback-ripple-pass-propose-next-cut]]).
- **Fields follow the story, left to right on one line; derived leftovers are not fields** (Frank, 2026-07-22, shares mockup, settled after three iterations): value per share → maximum shares → shares issued now → value of the shares issued now, one row, a unit suffix on every number ("shares" / "LSL"), example numbers chosen so no two figures coincide. A trivially derivable remainder is NOT shown as a field; its name lives in a help line. Never pair fields by formula across columns.
- **Drawn-section tags: two categories only, no field types for humans** (Frank, 2026-07-22): tags = **new** (blue) · **modified** (grey, with a "Was …" opening its help; absorbs renamed and repurposed — Frank picked the simpler, broader word). Field types (number, radio, file) never appear as visible tags: the box already says it (white dotted = applicant types, grey solid = the form fills), and the type survives for AI in a hidden data-type attribute. The required asterisk stays.
- **Mockups look like the REAL BPA and reflect the actual service — and the approved drawing IS the build contract** (Frank, 2026-07-22: "we will see exactly what will be the result, and this could be our contract"): pull the real section live (form_component_get), render it in the measured BPA canvas styles (Visual Dictionary "Spec visual verificada" + Rosetta Stone I/J; the bpa-visual-generator tool), then apply the change ON the real rendering. The only non-BPA overlay: the small new/modified chips anchored like BPA's own badges. Done = the built section indistinguishable from the contract drawing; the check = the same pipeline re-run on the built service, two renderings side by side. Full pattern: eR `knowledge/patterns.md` §"The faithful mockup is the build contract". Pilot: `services/register-a-business/specs/capital-shares-real-look.html`.
- **Canvas-true, logic included, full complexity** (Frank, 2026-07-22, from real BPA screenshots): the "where we stand" rendering matches the BPA canvas exactly: dashed outlines around every component, striped background where hidden, struck label + eye-slash where hideLabel, NO invented left bars; the REAL indicator badges (E effects, V validation, F formula, A action) at each component's own top-right corner, each badge with a plain-English tooltip on mouse-over AND a data-logic attribute for AI (today's logic, and for new components the logic to build). Never scope away a branch (the Preferred shares omission was the failure): the section's full complexity IS the situation.
- **background-red renders as a plain tinted box, NO left bar** (Frank, 2026-07-22): the red left bar belongs to a specific "notice" class that this service does not use; a `background-red` content is a red-tinted box only. Fold into the Visual Dictionary via the er-knowledge session.
- **Hidden fields are rendered too** (Frank, 2026-07-22): fields hidden from the applicant but feeding determinants (e.g. "Preferred shares are offered" true/false) appear in the drawing with the striped hidden look: "this is very important, so all should be rendered."
- **The logic's CONTENT is reviewable, not just badged** (Frank, 2026-07-22): behind every E/V/F badge, the actual content (what the formula computes, what the validation checks, what the effect shows/hides and on which determinant) is spelled out: in the tooltip where it fits, and IN ANY CASE in a collapsed "The logic, spelled out" annex under each drawing (one line per badge: component · type · plain English · ids), pulled live via determinant_get / componentformula_get / componentbehaviour_get_by_component. For the END situation the logic annex is part of what Frank agrees to: logic is contract like the pixels.
- **Contract-grade end situation** (Frank, 2026-07-22): chips mark EVERY changed element, content texts included (a new intro wears "new"). The author never comments inside the screen (no "this proposal draws the X path" notes): paths are SHOWN, one screen per path, chips only on what changes in each. Helps as short as possible without losing meaning. Every piece of logic in the end situation is specified like the pixels: badge + plain-English tooltip + data-logic saying exactly WHEN it fires and what replaces what (e.g. an error that appears only when issued value exceeds the capital), because the approved drawing is the implementer's roadmap.
- **The drawing shows today's reality and the change. Nothing else** (Frank, 2026-07-22, twice). No traces of previous design attempts, ever: an abandoned idea leaves no ghost, no "not a field" note, no negotiation history on the page (that trace cost a correction). A dashed struck-through ghost is honest ONLY for an element that exists in the live service today and that the change removes (e.g. the BO plan's three removed grids). Decision trails for AI go in the collapsed technical block, never in the drawing.
- **The mock carries the texts AND the changes** (Frank, 2026-07-22): when a drawing shows every explanation in place, no separate texts table; when it carries the change tags too (new · renamed · repurposed as small grey tags, a grey "Was …" line in the help, and a dashed struck-through GHOST outline for a field deliberately not shown), no change table either. Tables of this kind exist only where the drawings abbreviate. What a mock cannot draw (an operator change, = becoming ≤) gets its own small subsection.

- **One kit, one face (Frank, 2026-07-22, shares review).** A page carries ONE mockup kit: when a rendered import arrives, strip its `<style>` block and fold what it adds into the page's single `<style data-specimen>` kit block, rule order preserved. Two definitions of one class is the defect family that made "The check that changes" render in the wrong face. Every top-level section summary uses the same heading element (h2 in the page face); sub-details use plain text summaries.
- **Every rendering collapses; the index is a chip row (Frank, 2026-07-22).** Each screen sits in its own collapsed sub ("The screen today" · "Screen 1 · Only ordinary"); the legend sits above the subs and carries ONLY the non-obvious marks (striped hidden, logic circle, new/modified chips) — white, grey and dashed boxes are self-evident, never repeated. Commentary runs full width, no max-width column. The index nav is one row of pill chips with quiet inline group labels, never a stacked list. The "Where we stand" pill reads "the current screen".
- **The check-that-changes folds into the end situation (Frank, 2026-07-22).** Three plain sentences in a collapsed sub beat a four-row table; a standalone section only when the checks truly need their own space.
- **Triggers grouped by his categories, every row located (Frank, 2026-07-22: "whatever is in the screen, say changes in the screen; what is in the bot, put in the bot").** Groups: The screens (first a one-line pointer: the chips and badges on the contract screens ARE the change list; then only undrawn screens get rows) · The bots · The database · The paper · The words. Each row named by its bot, register, document or label; a one-line check never sits beside a whole bot as an equal. Label renames carry their translation-row need.
- **The badge tooltip carries the full logic, in the design vocabulary (Frank, 2026-07-22: "it would be ideal" · "we say determinant is this one, and effect is hide, show, enable").** Every drawing badge shows its complete logic sentence on mouse-over via a styled `data-tip` bubble, the same sentence as its annex row. An E badge names the determinant first, then the effect axes, then the default ("Determinant: Type of shares is Ordinary and preferred shares. Effect: Show + Enable + Activate. Default: hidden."); a V badge states the check; an F badge states the formula. Native `title` is not used anywhere on a drawing, badges or containers (it fights the styled bubble). The annex stays as the reviewable list; the tooltip is the fast path on the drawing itself.
- **Tooltips carry ONE core sentence, never status words (Frank, 2026-07-22: "what does kept unchanged mean? remove everywhere").** The unmarked element IS the kept one: chips mark change, so no "Kept unchanged" / "Kept as today" anywhere, tooltips or annex leads; annex rows open "Formula:" / "Validation, modified:" / "Formula, new, to build:" and the cap says unmarked = as today. A tooltip states only the core logic (determinant + axes + default for E; the check for V; the formula for F); commentary tails (drift notes, what replaces what, build reminders) live in the annex only. Information content components draw as a soft blue box (Frank: "this type of information content field could be in blue").
- **Phases are the session's own build plan: separate collapsible blocks, tests at the end, checked by Frank's eyes (Frank, 2026-07-22, shares plan, settled after three passes).** The AI implements. One WHITE collapsed `details` card per phase ("phases blocks would look better in white"); inside: the build actions as bullet points, one action per bullet ("separate the actions"), then the fixed closing lines, tests at the end: My test (the test IS the pass bar; a separate "Success criteria" line was tried and removed the same day, Frank: "more or less the same as the task, remove them" — the label "Done when" had already failed before it) · Sol's test (Frank: "ask gpt sol to also test after you" — the independent GPT tester via Codex, blind: contract screens + the phase's tests only, never the build notes; scripts, Playwright + REST, not MCP) · Your decision where one exists · Your check, ALWAYS with direct links to the exact BPA and GDB pages he opens (Frank: "to make my check easier, put the link to the concerned BPA or GDB pages"; only verified URL patterns). "So that we progress on solid bases and can correct early." A single 4-column table (phase | criteria | tests | check) was tried and ROLLED BACK the same day ("it was better when phases were separate"); if a table form ever returns, each phase must collapse. NEVER "gate" or "your word": write "Your decision (answer in the chat, I write it here)". Success criteria concrete, no artifacts he cannot picture ("fresh export saved" failed; "the drawing above matches today's tab exactly" passes). Single breaks, never double. A phase on an undrawn tab starts with its own pull + contract drawing + his verdict before building.
- **GDB vocabulary (Frank, 2026-07-22, same review):** name the database by its GDB name, "the GDB Legal Persons", not "the register" — in CHAT REPLIES as much as on pages (Frank, 2026-07-22: "I never talk about the register. Use my vocabulary."); FIELDS, not columns; new fields listed by name, never counted ("with two columns" earned the correction "why with two columns? you should list them"); a GDB draft version is not operational, the version the service needs is published.
- **Certificates, order, open questions, pre-build review (Frank, 2026-07-22, plan closing).** Say "the certificates", never "the paper(s)". In tensions with parked changes: say where the parked change lives (its document, linked) and name the build order — the phases decide who builds first, never "whoever builds second" ("we are masters of the phases"). A question Frank wants open stays WRITTEN in the document until he closes it. Before phase 0: an adversarial evidence-based review by two independent reviewers (a Claude agent on the live service read-only + Codex on the saved raw files), findings applied into the document, then his verdict, then the build.
- **The opening summary: short bullets, each a full plain sentence (Frank, 2026-07-22, two corrections the same evening):** first "be serious and build a decent text" (no telegraphic fragments like "changes one check, renames the total"), then "make it concise and in bullet points". Settled shape, one bullet each: what the form forces today (and that the law does not ask it) · what the change lets the company do · the fields that make it · the words that change · one caution if there is one. **The opening summary is a collapsible `<details id="short">` titled "The change in short", collapsed by default, holding the `.stamp` bullets inside; not an index chip (Frank, 2026-07-23: "make opening summary collapsible" + "see capital and shares, collapsed").** The reference form is the capital-shares plan; the bullets that used to sit in an always-visible box now collapse like every other section. All three plans (capital-shares, bo, preferred-shares) carry this identical form.
- **"Determinant", never its type; "validation", never "a check" (Frank, 2026-07-22):** prose says "determinant", not "numeric/radio determinant" (the type belongs to data-logic and the technical block); and "check" is not eRegistrations vocabulary — the thing is a determinant (on a message) or a validation (on a field). When a needed tool is missing, first try to rebuild the rule out of TOOLED parts (the equality validation became the field's own built-in "maximum taken from another field" + a determinant operator change) before resorting to hand work in the BPA screen.
- **Banned jargon in Frank-facing prose (Frank, 2026-07-22: "what does pull mean? ... what does stale mean?"):** never "pull" (say "I re-read the tab from the live service and save a dated copy"), never "stale" ("the drawing shows the service exactly as it is on build day"), never "export" ("the dated copy"), never "the team" bare ("the service designers"; and when re-reading is motivated by their edits, also say plainly that this plan's changes are all the session's own, through the MCP tools). These words stay fine only inside the collapsed for-AI technical block.
- **Token audit with canvas quotations (2026-07-22).** BPA-measured styles live in `<style data-specimen>` blocks; overlay-grammar inline styles carry `data-specimen`; the first style block stays token-pure and audited; dead grammar is deleted, not exempted.

## Phase-0 lessons (Frank, 2026-07-23, capital-shares build, line by line)

- **Plan and progress never mix.** A phase bullet is ONE short task sentence, frozen; progress is a separate green line under it: a small **done** chip (var(--ok), pill outline) + a few plain words + the date. Findings are NEVER written into the plan text ("I don't know what you have done, what you haven't done"). Detail goes to the method section or the technical block. Phase text one size smaller (--t-smd 0.9rem; done lines --t-sm).
- **Phase 0 holds only build-day preparations** (fresh dated copy, version/schema work, probes). Understanding the service is PLAN work and lives in the plan's sections: what a validation checks → the screens' logic annexes; which GDBs and bots the change reaches → "What the change triggers". Frank: "this is something which is done when we make the plan."
- **The reach question, asked his way: "which GDBs hold fields the change modifies?"** Method: all GDBs holding modified fields → for each, the bots writing those fields → the phase where each is modified. Exclusions stated with their reason (the GDB OWNERS holds share fields, but none is modified: the phase-3 change touches only hidden form totals no bot writes). A GDB is not involved because its topic matches; only because a modified field lives in it.
- **Field naming: the durable meaning names the GDB field AND the form label; the moment lives in the tooltip.** "Shares issued" everywhere ("Shares issued" GDB + "Shares issued now" form was tried first; Frank unified the same night: "Shares issued is clear"). Never bake "now" into an accumulating field.
- **Explanations ride as tooltips, not under the field** (Frank, 2026-07-23: "a bit complex, better as a tooltip"): description empty, tooltip carries the sentence; in the drawing a small circled i on the label with the data-tip bubble.
- **Citizen-text examples: a good one or none** (Frank, 2026-07-23: "raise more money: what does it mean?"): when the honest example would confuse (shares issued later at another nominal value), leave the example out rather than write a vague one.
- **Presets, never invented classes**: a content's look uses the system's own custom classes exactly as the human designers pick them (Background Blue = background-blue; verify against a sibling preset in the same service).
- **Build on the designers' open draft and publish**: fold additions into their draft, publish; nothing of theirs is lost. Frank overruled wait-and-ask ("if you start on the basis of the draft, then you add your additions and you publish, and nothing is lost").
- **Existing GDB draft versions are neglected (Frank, 2026-07-25, BO plan checkup: "update the GDB as needed and publish... these existing draft versions should be neglected").** A draft version someone else left on a GDB is never asked about and never waited for: build the new version and publish; what matters is that the final PUBLISHED schema is exactly the one the change needs. A draft version is not operational and nothing targets it; asking its opener parks the build. Twin of the open-draft rule above: the form design folds into their draft, a GDB draft is simply ignored.
- **Words**: "ceiling" banned in prose (say "at most the capital"; the alert is "the message that appears when the value goes over the capital"); "piece" → data field; "writer bot" → data bot. Every done line says what was done AND not done ("read only, nothing modified"), names the acting bot and its phase.
- **Reopen at the modified part, stamped in the file**: move `open` attributes onto the edited section's details chain before `open`-ing the page (URL fragments are not honored on reused browser tabs; a forced fresh load needs a changing ?v=N). All open stamps come off at final delivery.
- **A finished phase shows it on the closed card** (Frank, 2026-07-23: "it should appear also on the closed section"): a done chip inside the summary line, `<summary>0 · Preparations <span class="done"><b>done</b> your check passed, 23 July</span></summary>`; the open stamp moves to the phase now in build.
- **The drawing is as big as the change** (Frank, 2026-07-23, Shareholders tab: "We could have drawn just the part which is changing, not the whole page"): the READING is always the whole tab (it corrects false beliefs and locates the logic; the raw goes to the dated copy), but for a minor change the DRAWING shows only the changing part, AS A TODAY/CHANGED PAIR like the certificates (Frank: "comparing the current and the end situation, that's much clearer"), in just enough context to recognize it, with one quiet line "the rest of the page is unchanged". Full-page canvas drawings are for screens whose change is structural (the capital tab); never for a two-word swap.
- **A test delivers a FULL RESULTS REPORT, never "it works" (Frank, 2026-07-23):** the phase's test is a WRITTEN SCENARIO with concrete numbers and an expected result per line; the run then produces a report showing **expected against actual, line by line, pass or fail each**, with the registry/GDB values printed, the evidence (screenshots, file id) linked, and any deviation stated. Delivered as an HTML page beside the plan and opened for Frank. Frank reads the report, not a verdict sentence. A phase whose "test" is a vague sentence ("run the whole path once") is undefined — write the scenario.
- **Sub titles name the part of the system, one grammar** (Frank, 2026-07-23: "consistency in the titles with clear meaning. Relation to the part of the system we show"): "The <name> tab · <path or moment>" and "The <name> certificate · <part>" — "The Capital & shares tab · only ordinary", "The Articles certificate · the capital recap", "The Shareholders tab · the warning". Never "Screen 1" as a name; prose refers to "the only-ordinary drawing".
- **The end situation covers every visible element, drawings first** (Frank, 2026-07-23: "the end situation should consider all elements, not only the screens" + the order question): screens, certificates (today/changed pairs), further drawn tabs — all the drawings together at the top of the section; the logic annex and method subs after them.
- **Keys are never visible, anywhere on the page** (Frank, 2026-07-23: "No need to mention this key here. If it is for AI, find a way to hide it. Apply this rule to all places."): UUIDs ride in `data-key` attributes (or data-logic) — even the annex Ids column hides (`.idcell{display:none}`, content in data-key). A key next to a name in prose is a defect.
- **Stamps carry only what Frank needs** (Frank, 2026-07-23: "read-only... Remove. Apply this rule everywhere: remove what is not needed."): a stamp is "read live, 23 July 2026" — never "pulled", never "read-only", no profile names, no ids.
- **A check-and-act pair is one bullet** when it is one motion ("check what the GDB stores; add what is missing and publish") — split bullets read as two half-done states.
- **Resolve the database home by READING the GDBs, before writing the "database" trigger row (Frank, 2026-07-23, preferred-shares plan).** Never pose "store it in the GDB or not?" as an open question to Frank, and never accept a storage home (mine or Frank's) without reading the schema first. Enumerate the service's databases (`gdb_database_list`), read the company DB and the per-actor DBs the bots write to, and choose the home by the **grain of the data**: company/class-level facts live in the company DB (Legal Persons has `CAPITAL/Prefered shares`), per-shareholder facts in the shareholders DB (Owners/Shareholders), per-owner facts in the owners DB. The "What the change triggers → the database" row is grounded in that read, never left conditional. Failure that earned this: I posed the preferred-class rights as an open storage question AND accepted a wrong DB home without reading the schemas. Full lesson: eR `knowledge/lessons.md` §"Read the service's GDBs before proposing where new data is stored". Working-style twin: [[feedback-evidence-over-agreement]].

- **The summary line carries the verdict (Frank, 2026-07-25, BO logic-checked sub: "we should see on title line if fine or not, put indicators"; refined the same day: "must say more and reflect categories").** Any section that reports a checked state shows that state ON THE CLOSED LINE as small quiet outline chips: the TOTAL then the three-way split — "9 faults · 3 fixed · 4 for you · 2 designers" ("fixed" in the ok green); a clean scan shows "clean" in green; a test section its pass counts; a phase its done chip. Never a vague chip ("owners for you" alone failed). And the section BODY is organized by the SAME three groups, in order: FIXED (what existed → what was done) · NEEDS YOUR DECISION · FOR THE DESIGNERS; the fault family is a small grey tag per row, never the grouping. **The first group has a TENSE** (2026-07-25, the BO honesty note): before the build it is titled "THIS CHANGE FIXES" with a NEUTRAL grey chip (each row: today's fault → what the change does, in which phase); the green "fixed" title and chip appear only as phases land, row by row, each with its done line and audit — green is progress, never plan (the plan-vs-progress law applied to chips). The reader's eye gets the verdict without opening anything; the body carries only the detail.

## The end-to-end test is the last SECTION, not a phase (Frank, 2026-07-25: "this should not be a phase but the last section, final test")

A phase is a piece of building, checked before the next one starts. The end-to-end run is not that: it happens once, when the build is whole, and proves the whole change. So it leaves the phases block and becomes the document's closing section, "The final test", pill *the proof*, just before the technical block.

**How to apply:** per-phase tests stay inside their phases (they gate the next one); only the whole-change run is lifted. The phases section carries one line saying the end-to-end test is the final test, linked. Phase bodies that used to say "the proof rides with the phase 6 run" now link to the final test. The section order becomes: analysis (triggers, tensions, legal ground) → the phases → the final test → the technical block.

## Every element is a link (Frank, 2026-07-25: "we should put links to all elements")

Seeing the triggers table naming the GDB Legal Persons, the other GDBs and the certificates as plain bold text, Frank asked for links on all of them. The change document is a console: from any named element the reader opens the element itself in one click.

**How to apply:** the triggers table first (every row's element name is a link), then everywhere the element is the subject — drawing captions, the drawing summaries (a quiet "open in BPA" chip), the certificates, the databases, the bots, the labels row (to translations). Ids stay hidden in `data-key`; the visible text stays the plain name. Repeat mentions inside a section do not re-link; the first one does.

**Verified BPA routes** (read from the frontend's own route table, 2026-07-25), under `https://<bpa-host>/services/<serviceId>`: `/forms/applicant-form` · `/print-documents/<documentId>` · `/print-documents` · `/bots/<botId>` · `/bots` · `/settings/databases` · `/formiotranslate` · `/roles/<roleId>` · `/registrations/<registrationId>` · `/determinantstable` · `/fieldstable` · `/messagestable`. A GDB is `https://<gdb-host>/database/<numericId>`, the published version. **Never invent a route**: unverified ones are checked first, or replaced by the nearest verified parent page. Twin of the workspace rule [[feedback-cite-with-link]].

## One-screen changes: today and proposed side by side (Frank, 2026-07-22, internal-bot write mode)

When the change touches ONLY ONE screen, do not split it into "Where we stand" and "The end situation" sections. Show both states in ONE section, side by side (the mockup layout): left = Today with its plain-words consequence, right = Proposed (the contract), labels TODAY / PROPOSED above the panels. The two-section split remains for multi-screen plans.

**Why:** with a single screen the comparison IS the message; splitting it across two collapsed sections makes the reader carry the image in their head.
**How to apply:** merge into `<details id="screens">` with a `.duo` grid; keep the choices table, composing notes and "checks that change" sub below the pair.

## Announce the plan before you explain it (Frank, 2026-07-26) — EVERYWHERE

Frank's own way of explaining, and now the house pattern for every summary, every narration and every opening section: **say how many things there are and name them, then take them one by one.**

> Three things: what a company really decides, what our form forces instead, and what we change.
> First … Second … Third …

**Why it works:** the first line hands the reader an empty box with three slots, so every sentence after it has somewhere to go, and they know when the part ends. That is what makes it stick. Analytical order (facts in the order they exist) gives five facts and no shape.

**Two rules that make it synthetic, not analytical:**
- **Name the slots by their role, not their content**: today · instead · on the screen. Not "the capital field, the maximum field, the rule".
- **Say what is at stake before the mechanics.** The opening must answer "what is going on and what do we want to change", in the reader's world, not in the form's. The mechanics arrive last, as the consequence, where they are easy because the reason is already known. Frank: "It's not analytical, but should be a synthetic comment."

**And name the notions.** A change is hard to follow when one of its notions has no name. Give it the name the LAW uses (read the Act before choosing a word: Lesotho says "authorised shares", never "authorised capital"), then the whole story can be told in two words.

Applies to: the opening summary of every plan, every narration segment, the first line of a section that carries more than two ideas. Reference: the capital-and-shares summary and its narration, 26 July 2026.

## The top part: menu, title, one line, one box, chips (Frank, 2026-07-27)

Frank showed his own publishing concept note beside the shares plan and said the note's arrangement is the better one: *"This way of organizing the top part is better. The menu and below, so apply."*

Order, and nothing else above the first section:

1. the navigation bar FIRST, full width, sticky, one bottom border: brand · this page named · the operation's other pages · More for sources
2. the title
3. one qualifier line that says something ("Six phases, all built, proven from the citizen's screen to the registry row")
4. one provenance box: version, date, state, and the links
5. one row of index chips: the sections of this page

**Why:** the old head stacked a meta line, a backlink line, a summary and a menu before the reader reached anything. Page links belong in the bar, section links in the chips; mixing them made the bar long and the head tall.

## "Change in short" says today, then what we want (Frank, 2026-07-27)

## A small bold title on every block (Frank, 2026-07-27)

*"Let's make all that we write systematically and visually easy to understand. Put bold at the end of each sentence or group of sentences, as a small title."*

Each sentence or group of sentences opens with a two or three word **bold title**, then a full stop, then the sentences. **Today.** · **What the law asks.** · **What we want.** · **Where it lands.** · **Build order.** It goes with the announcement line, it does not replace it.

**Why:** the titles are read first. Five bold words give the shape of the argument before a single sentence is read.

**How to apply:** every bullet list, every paragraph group, every change entry. Bold inside a sentence stays reserved for a named notion. Full rule: [[feedback-bold-small-title-per-block]].
