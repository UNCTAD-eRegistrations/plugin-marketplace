# The leaflet editing spirit

**The rule over all rules (Frank, 23-08-2026). Say the thing.** Never present the page, comment on it, or tell how it was made. Fewest, simplest words; a word that changes nothing for the reader is cut. One page holds what one head holds; more goes to another page. Titles name the thing in the reader's words.

Distilled by Frank + session, 2026-07-21, from ~30 micro-corrections fine-tuning the Lesotho beneficial-owners leaflet. Applies to **every citizen leaflet and public page, every country**. The per-skill content rules say WHAT to write; this says HOW to edit it. Apply while writing, then once more as a dedicated re-read of the whole page.

## Precise: the exact word, on the exact spot

- Verbs name the real act: a company **records / registers / declares facts**; nobody "finds" or "tracks" people. Watch the verb's object: "declare these people" reads like cargo → "register these people".
- **The simplest everyday phrase wins**, even over formal correctness or matching the form's own question: "hold the shares for themselves", not "act on their own behalf".
- The essential axis rides one adverb, not a clause: "even when the shares are held through another company" → "**indirectly**, through another person or another company" (mirror of "directly owns more than 10%").
- A definition binds to the noun it touches, with a comma: "a director may be **a nominee, acting for someone else**". The appositive sits on the actor, never floating where it could attach to the other person.
- **No em dashes, ever** (standing rule, all outputs): bind an appositive with a comma; split a heavy sentence with a colon or a period.
- Form questions speak in the third person (the person filling the form is not the subject): "Does this shareholder hold the shares for themselves, or for someone else?", never "Are these shares yours".
- The data shows its key distinction inline: "Owns more than 10% **(directly)**" · "through Maluti **(indirectly)**".

## Bare: remove what does not add

- Say a thing once; the positive covers the negative: "any one of the four", delete "you do not need all four".
- A rule opens with its condition: "If a shareholder is another company, you must also register…", no scene-setting sentences before the point.
- Never narrate what the next sentence or the screen mock already shows; the mock is part of the text. A part intro is not restated by the part body.
- No answer-nudging asides ("Usually not."), no reassurance tails ("they are not hiding…"), no meta-tags that plant doubt ("added in practice"): true extra-legal items are simply listed, uncited.
- A requirement lives inside the box the eye scans, not in a paragraph after it.

## Expressive: carry the meaning to the eye

- When two lists could seem to contradict (four conditions vs five ways), one line names what each IS: the conditions are **why** a person counts; the ways are **where** the form records them.
- Bold whole meaning units, never word confetti: "**directly owns more than 10%**", "**any one of the four**".
- Check every metaphor for insinuation: "behind" whispers hiding, "journey" inflates; prefer "through", "process". Keep an idiom only where its common sense is the intended one (a hero title "the people behind the company" is fine: there it means the ones who make it).
- State purposes in the positive: "the registry always knows who really owns and controls each company", not "not hidden ownership".
- **For the ear: a spoken list announces its shape and numbers its beats** ("in two ways. First… Second…"), with the beats named exactly as the page's subheads. The eye sees symmetry in subheads; the ear cannot.

## The method that carried it

- **The ripple pass (Frank, 2026-07-21): every applied change triggers a re-read of its surroundings with the three moves, and the next cut is PROPOSED, not waited for.** A merged sentence often makes its neighbor redundant; the editor who applied the change is the one who sees it first. Waiting for Frank to spot it is the failure.

- Every change logged **with its why** in a per-leaflet change note (`<leaflet>-changes.md`), the report to the counterpart.
- Every wording decision **mirrored into the service build plan the same hour** (plural persons → a repeatable grid; real menu names; the question wording). The leaflet is the design instrument: its promise is the build contract.
- **Text final before voice.**
- Deadlines and claims **re-verified in the statute before sharpening** them (reg. 7/8/12 were read before "7-day" and "at least 10 years" went in).
