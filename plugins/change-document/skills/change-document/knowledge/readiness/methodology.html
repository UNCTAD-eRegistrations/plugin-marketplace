Readiness of a change document: the two-stage methodology

14-09-2026, from the two houses' reports (Claude house, Codex Sol), the lessons page and Astra's two turns. Frank's frame and names: change doc review (stage 1), change build review (stage 2).

Corrected 15-09-2026 by the build itself. Phase 0 of the reference case, run on draft, met 22 faults and 19 of them no review had foreseen. Frank's measure: every fault nobody foresaw must change the method so it would have caught it. The corrections are folded into the sections below, each naming its fault; the evidence is the build ledger.

What a readiness review is, and is not

A change document says, with drawings as the contract, what an eRegistrations service looks like today, what it looks like after the change, and how a service designer gets from one to the other in the BPA screens without code. Readiness is two questions asked in order. Stage 1, the document: is it true, consistent and complete, so that everyone who reads it, the citizen, the officer, the registry, the platform, the builder, the Director, sees the same service? Stage 2, the build: can each drawn element be made in BPA as drawn, from a live model; what does each change touch elsewhere in the service and in the instance; and does the plan build and test every element in an order that works, up to live and back?

Stage 2 does not start before stage 1 hands over a settled page: a build review of a page still moving is redone. Authority. The reviewers read: the page, and the live instance with read tools only; they change nothing, run no probe, publish nothing. The page session that owns the document verifies every finding live before applying it, applies the document's repairs, publishes the document and proves it served, and keeps the refuted findings with their evidence. Probes on draft are the builder session's, under its own authority, and their results return to the page. Nobody changes the method's criteria: a disposition goes to Frank as one page of lettered choices. Two things the build taught about authority itself. The skills and lessons the house already holds are read before the first file, so a matter already ruled on is not raised again as a finding. And a change on a live system handed to a background run cannot show its authorisation: the run is flagged however plainly the owner approved it in the session, so such a change is made attended, or the authorisation and its date travel inside the brief and the flag is expected. Both stages presume the parent skill's preparation before any drawing: the change's size, the decisions dated, the owner's facts, the populations measured, the criteria and the platform rules read.

The element classes, the spine both stages walk

Every check below names its class. The list is the shared knowledge of the two skills; a review that skips a class says so in "What I could not verify".

  1. Screens per outcome: the citizen screens as the person sees them, one per outcome of each path, and the BPA canvases, every component once.
  2. Fields: added; removed, renamed or type changed; their labels, keys and defaults; hidden fields with their filler and their readers, the file's listing value among them (what the officer's list shows a file by, and the sources the copy device reads in order).
  3. Determinants and effects: the condition, its source field and the stored answer, the empty-state answer, the component acted on, the three axes (Activate/Deactivate, Show/Hide, Enable/Disable) against the component's Default behavior, the ancestors.
  4. Validations: the rule, the message, the moment it is checked (a line's Save, the page, Send).
  5. Formulas and copy devices: the operator, the source, the moment it recomputes, what reads the result.
  6. Grids: the fields of a line, the root or nested counter, the multiple bots over the grid, what an internal bot does to the list.
  7. Bots and mappings: data bots (read, list, exists, update, create, update or create, entries), internal bots, document bots, message bots; each with its GDB table and version, its key, its input and output mappings, its action row and BotRole.
  8. GDB tables: columns, versions (draft and live are two GDBs), views, triggers, GDB-sourced catalogues that pin a version, the set of entries shared across versions, and for each store whether it is mirror-managed and from where (a mirrored store refuses a schema change with 403 and is changed at its origin; a plan that says "create it on draft" for one is unbuildable, and the build is where that is discovered).
  9. Catalogues and subcatalogs: entries, keys and values, groups, the parent a copied select points to.
  10. Registrations, roles, BotRoles (say role, never desk, Frank, 15-09-2026; every role the plan needs is in the flow and active, and a role left under Other roles is a finding, not a feature): the registration's own anatomy, its subjects, its result, its requirements in data, documents and fees, and the unit in charge (an empty Registrations tab is a fault, not a detail); the activation key, the routing conditions, the destinations per button and status, the institution link, the processing start role, the users who hold a role; a BotRole holds its bots or refuses to publish.
  11. Certificates, messages and translations: the fields and columns they show, their templates, the moment they fire, the translation lines every new text needs. Messages have their own group on the page (Frank, 15-09-2026): each names the role and the status that sends it, and each email is drawn as received. A message sits on a status of an existing role (the receipt on File pending, the refusal on File rejected, the approval after the registry writes) or on a bot action on a button; a role created only to send a message is a finding unless the page gives its reason.
  12. Fees and payments, even when none.
  13. Shared surfaces: My Dashboard and any screen every citizen opens; the cost and the failure mode of what is placed there.
  14. The old GDB tables (the Legacy stores) and the facts of the old system, written by their owner, dated.
  15. The plan: phases, sizes, publish steps, tests on new files, probes with fallbacks, decisions with owners, go-live, regression, the way back.
  16. The moment: when a validation or a determinant reads a field against when the bot or the formula fills it; when a formula on a role form computes against when the button is pressed.
  17. Access (added 15-09-2026, faults 1, 2, 3, 13, 14): the servers each tool call goes through and their versions, the profile of every instance the plan names and what kind of thing each profile is, the accounts each phase needs (the citizen as well as the officer), and the tools the plan depends on. Nothing in this class is visible on a drawing, and phase 0 of the reference case lost a day to it.

Stage 1: change doc review

Question. Is the document true, consistent and complete? When. Once the drawings are settled and Frank's screen review is applied; again after each pass of repairs, as a closure. Who. Two houses from the same brief, read only, each writing one findings file; the page session merges, verifies and applies. Reads. The page as published (its version recorded), the decisions list, the facts of the old system, the one-pager and every sibling page; the live service only to establish the current situation (what exists today). Stage 1 checks the mappings, versions and catalogues as the page SPECIFIES them, against the page's own grids; stage 2 proves them against the live instance. If the current situation cannot be established, the review says so and withholds the verdicts that rest on it.

Procedure.

  1. Freeze the evidence set. Record the page's published version and address, the decisions of the sessions, the facts of the old system, the one-pager, the date of the last live read of the current service. Stop if the page version or the authority of a decision is uncertain. Every limitation met while gathering the evidence goes into the plan's technical anchors, not only into the evidence file (faults 3 and 4): where a tool could not resolve a name, the anchors carry the id and say the name is inferred, so no builder reads an inference as a read fact.
  2. Inventory the contract. Count once every screen, canvas, component, hidden field, determinant, effect, validation, formula, action, bot, GDB table and version, catalogue, role form, registration, BotRole, certificate, message, translation, fee. The machine counts what it can (logic check, screen labels check); a human finds the promises that have no letter and no line. Count again after every change a review makes (fault 5): moving a device moves the counts, so the roles, BotRoles and destinations are recounted whenever a bot or a message moves, and every message the plan places names the role it fires from.
  3. Current against end situation. The current drawing against the live screen; each current element against its end-state element, changed or untouched; every decision against every summary sentence, canvas note, component note, effect row, bot description, phase line and test line. Stop on a contradiction between two controlling sentences.
  4. The two drawings and the logic lists. Every citizen label exists on its canvas; every E, V and A letter has its line; the counts match the drawings (the checks exist as tools); one copy of everything, no hidden pane, no second version of a section. A human reads what the tools cannot: an undrawn case, a plausible wrong sentence, a layout BPA would not draw.
  5. The decision sweep by words. For every decision that removed a rule (a mask, a question, a road), search the whole page for the words and examples of the withdrawn rule, tips and notes included, and list every place before the first edit. This is the pitfall that survived three reviews on the case.
  6. Conditions in the empty state. For every determinant: source field, stored answer, empty answer, component, axes against the Default behavior, ancestors. Stop if an empty bot answer exposes Send, a required field stays active inside a deactivated panel, or an effect cannot reach its component.
  7. Fields and outcomes. For every live GDB entry the change creates, each column is taken from an old store, asked, copied, computed or a mapping constant, against the page's own GDB grid. Every hidden field names its filler and its readers. Every outcome has a citizen screen, a canvas condition, an officer route where one applies, a final-test line. The state grid, the reviewer's own: every path against every outcome, and the cases that hide between them, a duplicate, an orphan entry, a repeat recovery, a bot stopping half-way, each with the state of Send and the route that repairs it. Fees and payments, even when none, and the officer's workload are decisions recorded here; stage 2 checks their devices and capacity. The registration's anatomy is part of the contract (fault 18, found by Frank in the BPA screens): what it delivers, which documents it asks and under which conditions, its fees and its unit in charge, each specified on the page and built in a named task. An empty Registrations tab fails this stage. The file's listing value is specified too (fault 19): the officer's list shows a file by its application name, so the page names the hidden field, the sources its copy device reads and their order.
  8. People, evidence and words. Seven personas walk the page: the citizen holding an old certificate, the officer, the registry, the platform with its rules, the plan, the builder, and the third party whose record the service may expose. The citizen's sentences say what to do, what to give and what happens next; the officer's role form shows what the decision rests on; every number, name, code, count and legal claim traces to its authority; the vocabulary, slop and token checks run on the page and on the reviewers' replies.
  9. The plan's coverage. Every element of the inventory is built in a named step, every outcome has its action, expected screen, role, message and named GDB values in the final test. The phase-coverage check finds the names absent from the phases; a name present is not yet a build step, so the human reads the step. The phases are read as grids: one line per task with its test, each fault line marked fixed, worked around or open, the phase title carrying its count, and the page's fault count equal to the build ledger's. A fault counted in the summary with no openable line is a gap. Order and publish steps are stage 2's.
  10. The moment, read on paper. Where a validation or determinant reads a field a bot fills, the page says which comes first; where a role formula gives a date, the page says the day it records.
  11. The findings file and the hand-off. Findings numbered, grouped (consistency, cases, people's words, evidence, plan coverage), each with a scenario, its severity and what it does to readiness, the places that conflict or, for an omission, the promise and the missing element, the authority, a one-line repair, the stage it belongs to. Then "What I could not verify" and the verdicts withheld because of it. The hand-off to stage 2: the frozen page version, the inventory, the findings resolved and refuted, the open authorities, the case list, the "could not verify" items, so that nothing is lost between the stages.

Refuses. To change the service or run a probe under a reviewer's authority; to give an unconditional readiness verdict while a controlling decision, an incomplete repair or a consequential probe is unresolved; to accept a mixed citizen-and-BPA drawing, or an effect that contradicts its independent axes; to review a page whose version is not recorded; to approve a sentence because it is plausible; to pass a registration whose result, documents, fees and unit are nowhere on the page.

Stage 2: change build review

Question. Can it be built as drawn, what does each change touch, and does the plan get there and back? When. After stage 1's hand-off; again as a closure after repairs; the plan chapter again after any restructure of the phases. Who. Two houses from the same brief with the BPA and GDB tools on the instance, read only; a house whose readers do not connect says so in its first line and falls back to dated copies, marked. Reads. Everything live, with a reads log kept: the models named on the page, the mappings, the latest published version of every GDB table named and the version each bot and catalogue pins, the catalogues, the roles and institution of a live service of the same shape, the instance-wide use of every GDB table the change versions; first the connection of the read tools and the instance's platform version against the rules' RECHECK list. A dated copy never stands in for a live read: what could not be read is listed, and the verdicts that rest on it are withheld.

Step zero, access, before the first probe (faults 1, 2, 3, 6, 10, 11, 13, 14). Every server the plan names starts and reports its version. Every profile the plan names exists, answers its connection status, and is the kind of thing the plan calls it: on the reference instance there is no draft BPA, only a draft DS and GDB, and half a sentence in a handover cost a session its afternoon. The second reviewing house reaches the same instances with the same server version, not only its own. Every tool the plan depends on is called once here, so a tool that cannot run is known before it is needed. The accounts each phase needs, the citizen's as well as the officer's, exist and can do their act. And the entries of every GDB table the probes read are counted on the instance the build will run on: a plan verified against live says nothing about draft, where both Legacy stores held zero entries. A claim that something does not exist is scoped to where it was read, and repeated on the build instance. A cached failure is a symptom to diagnose, never a wait. When the build instance signs in through a live realm (the reference instance's draft uses realm LS, shared with live, and the Keycloak profile lesotho-draft reaches that same realm, read 16-09-2026), no session creates a test identity there: a test identity in a live realm is a production identity. The lines that need a second citizen or a separate officer are written BLOCKED in the test record, never weakened to the one account available, and the identity strategy goes to Frank as a decision (issue 641).

Chapter A, each device. For every component, determinant, effect, validation, formula, copy device and bot: the BPA device, the live model (a component key or a bot name read this day), the platform rule that governs it, the status: buildable as drawn · probe (named) · not as drawn (with the repair in the page's words) · left to guess (a mapping's source, a condition value, a hidden field's filler, a catalogue unnamed). A model's name is read off the product, never assumed from the page. A bot's mappings belong to the bot: one bot per level, never one bot filling a grid field and a root field. For every field: its Part A page (guide, applicant form, documents, payment, send page), its registration flag, what resets it, the source and destination of each mapping that names it, its survival through approval (a document's id embedded at every approve).

Every write the plan needs is proved on a throwaway item first, and the item is read back (fault 7): a tool that answers success while dropping a field shows it only in the read-back. Found that way on the reference case: the tool that updates a status destroys a button's destination and cannot set one, which becomes a named hand step in the BPA screens rather than a surprise in the build. Where a tool refuses, the refusal is re-read against the tool's own schema before it is called a defect or routed around (fault 22): the call is retried with only the values being changed, because a tool that types its parameters fills in the ones it is not given. A brief never says "pass the current values back unchanged", and a typed tool is never bypassed with a raw call on a live registry.

Chapter C, the plan. The order as prerequisites, never a fixed sequence: a component before the bot that fills it, a field before the determinant that reads it, a bot before the action row that fires it, a role before the approval that goes through it, a publish before the test that needs it; sizes as counts per phase (bots, determinants, formulas, validations), the estimates being the builder's; a publish to draft before every test and the test on a file created after it; the roles, institution and processing start role created before the first publish; the routing conditions named with their answers; the destinations per button; the sizes per phase; the probes placed in the phase that builds their device, each with a passing result, a failing result and a fallback; the decisions with owners and dates; GDB versions created on draft and created again on live, the pinned bots moved; go-live in stops, one per publish, a regression after each, the census of the instance before; the way back rehearsed and timed, and where none exists (a GDB version), a forward repair named; the real recovery on live with its holder and its own way back; who is told and when.

What the platform refuses to publish orders the phases as much as what the citizen sees (faults 8, 9, 12). A BotRole publishes only with its bots, so it belongs to the phase that gives them, and every publish before that phase is planned without it. A registration created with a service id is not attached until it is linked, so every create is read back, the links it appears to have made included. An activation key can be read only off a file that carries the form, so the step that reads one names the file, its phase and the form it carries; an empty shell has nothing to read. The plan's order is its execution order (Frank, 14-09-2026): no task precedes a task it depends on, a long background task sits where nothing waits on it, and a task that may overlap says so. A machine check can flag a task whose prose names a later task as its input. The accounts are prerequisites, not scenery (fault 13): the citizen account and the officer account on the build instance are phase 0 tasks with owners, each proved able to do its act before the phase that needs it.

A phase is a session's natural unit, and the plan says so (Frank, 15-09-2026). No session holds a whole build: context runs out, and a session that starts in the middle of a phase inherits a half-built screen. So each phase ends with its publish, its test, its check and a handover, and that handover is the next session's brief. The coordinator of the stream watches for it and opens the next session itself, without being asked and never through the outgoing session, with a brief written from that handover rather than from a generic launch prompt: the ids proven so far, what is the owner's decision and not the builder's, the traps the build has already paid for, the next result to deliver and the stop conditions. The plan names those boundaries, so the build does not discover them by running out of room. Machinery and the coordinator's duties: the coordination-board skill.

Probes. A device no rule and no live model proves gets a probe: device · draft action · passing result · failing result · fallback · evidence kept. A failed probe selects its fallback or stops the build; a probe never becomes a test of the finished service. A platform rule the plan leans on is tested through the real execution path, not through a tool whose scoping differs (fault 20): a version-pinned count under-reported a live GDB table by two thirds and read as proof that a new version blinds the registry, while a read through the bot's own path showed a bot spans the store and sees every entry. Count unpinned, or read the way the device reads. A candidate correction to a platform rule goes to that knowledge's owner as a proposal, with its evidence, before the shared page is edited. When the house's own records contradict each other, a throwaway item settles it before anything that matters is written: three records disagreed about how GDB versions hold entries, and a two-entry store answered in a minute what would otherwise have been decided by inference over 53,000 entries.

Closure. After the repairs, the same reviewers reread their own list and the changed elements, then compare each repaired case across the citizen screen, the canvas, the logic list, the phases, the final test and the one-pager. An incomplete repair reopens its finding; a repair that changes the contract (a drawing, a decision) returns to stage 1 and to Frank when the drawing was his; dependent work waits for the closure. Readiness on draft and readiness for live are two verdicts: the second needs the probes' results, returned by the builder session, and the decisions closed. The mapping-level faults of the case surfaced only here.

The findings file and the hand-off to the builder. Five verdict lines (walked · buildable as drawn · probe · not as drawn · left to guess), the grid by canvas then bots, the impact record, the probes, the plan's findings by phase, "What I could not verify". The hand-off: the readiness grid, the probes with fallbacks, the impact record, the build order with its publish steps, the final-test cases, the go-live order, the closure list.

Refuses. To change the service or run a probe under a reviewer's authority; to give an unconditional readiness verdict while a controlling decision, an incomplete repair or a consequential probe is unresolved; to start on a page stage 1 has not handed over; to name a model it did not read that day; to assume a column absent because the id it was given lacks it (read the catalogue's version list); to let a dated copy stand for a live read; to call the plan buildable while a probe's fallback is unnamed; to let a test approve through a role that does not exist yet; to record a tool refusal as a defect before re-reading the tool's schema and retrying with only the values being changed; to say a tool cannot see something before testing that claim against a capability already exercised elsewhere; to treat a version-pinned count as a measure of what a bot sees; to open a probe before step zero's access is proved.

The briefs

One brief per pass, given to both houses, carrying: the page's version and address; the reading order; the facts of the old system and the instruction to attack every shape sentence with an old certificate in hand; the decisions, dated; the live ids of the day (the latest version of every GDB table by its catalogue, never a pinned id); the read tools allowed and the words "read only"; the output format; the success criteria. The five briefs of the reference case are the templates: the adversarial read of the document, the builder's pass, the closure, the adversarial read of the plan, the method's own reading.

The checks, as tools

Existing in the kit: screen-labels-check.py (every citizen label on its canvas), logic-check.py (letters, bot names, counts against the lists), phase-coverage.py (the names of the inventory absent from the phases; it fails when it finds no inventory, and a name present is not yet a build step), vocabulary-check.py, slop-check.py, token-audit.py, screen_truth.py (the current live screen). To build: decision coverage (every decision found on the page, its withdrawn words absent); state and outcome coverage (every outcome with a screen, a condition, a test line); plan order (no test before its device, a publish before each test); GDB usage census (instance-wide, every bot and catalogue pinning a GDB table); certificate, message and translation impact. A check that cannot run fails, never skips.

Where it came from

The second source is the build.
